Purpose & Performance — Quantifying the Relationship Between Purpose and Performance

RAG-based scoring · Regression analysis · S&P 500 · Capstone Team Project (Paul Merage School of Business, UC Irvine)

The Question

Do purpose-driven companies actually perform better financially, or is "purpose" mostly marketing language? Our team set out to quantify organizational purpose across all 500 S&P 500 companies using public data, build a transparent scoring index, and test whether higher purpose scores correlate with stronger financial results.

Why the S&P 500

The S&P 500 gave us a rigorous, widely trusted sample: standardized SEC reporting for data quality and consistency, publicly accessible filings for all 500 companies, and enough cross-industry diversity to test whether purpose drives performance broadly or only in certain sectors.

We pulled from four sources: SEC EDGAR (10-K filings), LinkedIn (company profiles and workforce data), S&P 500 index data, and official company websites — 100% publicly available data across all 500 companies.

How We Scored Purpose

We built a RAG-based scoring system that reads a company's disclosures and website content and evaluates it across three dimensions, each scored 0–5 by an LLM (Claude Opus) combined with an evidence-quality factor and an overlap penalty to prevent inflated scores from recycled citations:

  • Purpose Articulation — Is there an explicit purpose statement beyond profit? Is the language concrete and outcome-oriented, and does it appear in strategy documents rather than just marketing copy?
  • History Consistency — Has the purpose narrative stayed stable over the past 10 years?
  • Strategy Alignment — Do R&D spending and capital allocation actually reflect the stated purpose, and are operational decisions aligned with it?

The three dimensions combine into a final 0–100 purpose score. Performance combined EBITDA margin and ROIC minus WACC, run through a regression that strips out company size and industry effects, then time-weighted across multiple years of data.

Key Findings

The headline result: a statistically significant but weak positive correlation between purpose and performance across the full S&P 500 sample. That correlation was not uniform — when we broke it down by industry, Financials stood out sharply (n=70, coefficient 0.65, p=0.0007, R²=0.15), while most other industries (Consumer, Industrials, Health Care, Utilities, Information Technology) showed no statistically significant relationship at all. We also tested predictive power — does this year's purpose score predict next year's performance, or performance 5 years out? Both tests showed a weak, widely scattered positive relationship, not yet strong enough to stand alone as a predictor.

More: Outlier Studies, Limitations, Recommendations

Outlier Studies

Mastercard scored well above the sample mean on both purpose (45.7 vs. a mean of 24.3) and performance (98.5, the highest among top Financials) — consistent with our broader finding that in a sector built on trust, regulation, and long-term stakeholder relationships, purpose-driven practices may reflect stronger governance and lower reputational risk.

Qualcomm showed the opposite pattern: a very low purpose score (2.33/100) despite strong performance (61.95/100, above the IT industry median). The gap traced back to a real limitation in the scoring approach — Qualcomm's technical and legal filing language doesn't read like a mission-driven narrative, so the NLP-based rubric under-detected purpose signal that might genuinely be present in how the company operates. Limitations

  • Limited to 500 companies, which caps the breadth of the analysis
  • The purpose-scoring system only awards high scores when evidence is explicit and rubric-aligned — it can miss purpose that's expressed in less conventional language
  • U.S.-only scope (S&P 500), excluding global markets
  • Growth-stage and mature firms are scored on the same scale, losing some developmental nuance

Recommendations

  • Use purpose as one input among several, not a standalone predictor — pair it with operational metrics like business model viability and management quality
  • In Financials specifically, purpose appears to be a meaningful evaluation signal given the sector's reliance on trust and governance
  • A low purpose score doesn't necessarily mean a weak company, especially for IP-licensing or R&D-heavy firms whose language the NLP rubric under-detects — these cases warrant human review before drawing conclusions

Tech Stack

  • Python (regression analysis, data processing)
