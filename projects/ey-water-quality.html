EY Data Challenge — Clean Water Supply Optimization

Remote Sensing RandomForest Feature Engineering scikit-learn Python

The Problem

Predict three water-quality indicators — total alkalinity, electrical conductance, and dissolved reactive phosphorus — at around 140 sampling sites in South Africa, using satellite imagery and climate data. Ground measurements are slow and expensive to collect at scale; if spectral bands and climate variables can predict these indicators well, water quality could be monitored far more widely.

Approach

I built the spectral and climate feature-engineering layer on top of Landsat bands (NIR, green, SWIR16, SWIR22, NDMI, MNDWI) and TerraClimate PET, then tested whether the new features actually earned their place:

  • Interaction features — NDMI×MNDWI, PET×NDMI, PET×MNDWI, and PET crossed with individual bands
  • Ratio features — SWIR22/NDMI, NDMI/MNDWI, SWIR22/PET, green/SWIR22, SWIR16/SWIR22
  • Ablation study — trained a separate RandomForest per target with each feature added on top of a fixed baseline, comparing R² across all three targets
  • Modeling — RandomForestRegressor per target with feature scaling and a train/test split

Results

  • Test R² around 0.53–0.58 across the three targets, versus 0.88–0.92 on training — a clear overfitting gap given only ~140 samples
  • Most engineered features moved R² by less than a point in either direction — the baseline spectral and climate set had largely hit its ceiling

Reflections

This was my first hands-on work with remote-sensing data rather than tabular business data — pulling spectral indices out of satellite imagery and pairing them with climate variables. The habit I kept from it: test one feature at a time against a fixed baseline, rather than adding a pile of features and hoping the model sorts it out.
