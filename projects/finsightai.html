FinSightAI — Financial Document Intelligence Copilot

RAG · LLM Q&A · Risk Classification · SQL · Team Project (BANA 275, UC Irvine)

🔗 View on GitHub

The Problem

SEC filings like 10-Ks routinely exceed 100 pages, packing dense financial, operational, and risk information into unstructured text. Key information is scattered across many sections, which makes both manual review and automatic extraction difficult. Analysts end up spending hours reading filings one at a time — a process that doesn't scale when you're tracking many companies, and professional tools that solve this can cost $10K+ per year.

Across our dataset — the top 50 S&P 500 companies, 5 years of filings (2019–2025) — that's 250+ documents at roughly 120 pages each, with risk disclosures spread across 15+ sections per filing.

What We Built

FinSightAI is an AI-powered document intelligence platform with five core features: a RAG-based Q&A tool that answers plain-English questions with citations, a multi-company comparison engine, a 5-year trend analyzer, a financial metrics dashboard backed by SQL, and an automatic risk classifier — all powered by Google Gemini.

My Contribution — QA System & Risk Classification

I led the user-facing intelligence layer of the pipeline: the part that takes a retrieved set of document chunks and turns them into a trustworthy answer.

  • Built the LLM question-answering pipeline that generates grounded answers from retrieved SEC filing passages
  • Implemented citation validation so every answer traces back to a specific source filing and section, rather than an unverifiable claim
  • Designed a risk classification system that labels retrieved content into liquidity, market, regulatory, and operational risk categories
  • Worked on hallucination prevention — keeping generated answers constrained to what the retrieved evidence actually supports
  • Connected the full path from retrieval → LLM → final answer, so the QA and comparison features could reuse the same grounded-answer logic

How It Works

The system runs as a five-stage pipeline:

  • SEC EDGAR — pulls filings for 50 S&P 500 companies across 5 years
  • Text Extraction — converts HTML filings to clean text, chunked at ~1,200 characters
  • Embeddings — MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings indexed in FAISS for fast similarity search
  • RAG Retrieval — section-aware top-K search that returns the most relevant passages with citations
  • Gemini AI — generates the final grounded answer from the retrieved evidence

As a bonus feature, the team also built a SQL database with 15 structured financial fields (total revenue, net income, total assets, total liabilities, cash and equivalents, and more) extracted per filing, across all 50 companies and 5 years — which powers the dashboard and trend views.

Evaluation

We evaluated retrieval quality directly rather than assuming it worked:

  • Recall@5: 0.68 — the fraction of correct company chunks appearing in the top 5 retrieved results
  • MRR: 0.68 — mean reciprocal rank of the first correct chunk
  • Section Hit Rate: 63.3% — how often the correct 10-K section was retrieved for a given query type

These numbers matter because they're what makes the citations trustworthy — the QA layer I built is only as good as the evidence it's grounded in.

More: Comparison & Trend Features, Reflections

Multi-Company Comparison & Trend Analysis

Beyond single-document Q&A, FinSightAI can retrieve from multiple companies at once and synthesize a structured comparative analysis with an analyst takeaway — and track how language, risks, and financials evolved across all 5 years of data, including detecting risks that newly appeared or faded over time (for example, AI-related risk language showing up in 2022 filings but not in 2019). Reflections

What I Learned

  • How to keep an LLM's output constrained to retrieved evidence rather than letting it fill gaps from parametric memory
  • Practical citation validation — checking that a generated claim actually maps back to a specific retrieved passage
  • Designing a multi-class risk classifier that has to work across very different writing styles (a tech company's risk section reads very differently from a bank's)
  • Coordinating a shared retrieval → generation interface with teammates building the ingestion and comparison layers

Tech Stack

  • LLM: Google Gemini 2.5 Flash
  • Embeddings: MiniLM-L6-v2 + FAISS
  • Data: SEC EDGAR API
  • Database: SQLite
  • Frontend: Streamlit

Why This Project Matters

This project is a direct expression of what I want to keep building: systems that make LLM output verifiable, not just plausible. Anyone can wire an LLM up to a document and get an answer — the harder and more valuable part is making sure that answer is actually grounded in evidence, labeled with the right risk category, and traceable back to its source. That's the layer I owned here.
