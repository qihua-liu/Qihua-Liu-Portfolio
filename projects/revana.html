The Problem

Amazon sellers spend hours reading reviews one by one to find patterns software can surface in seconds. Existing tools return little more than a star rating — nothing on what to fix, what competitors do better, or what's driving returns.

Approach

Revana takes an Amazon product ID and returns a full product strategy in about 90 seconds. I worked on the stages that decide which reviews the AI is allowed to see — review fetching and fake-review filtering — plus sentiment-trend tracking and UI responsiveness:

  • Trust scoring — each review scored 0–100 across ~11 signals (verified purchase, length, posting bursts, linguistic patterns, reviewer history); anything below 50 is dropped before analysis
  • Review fetching — multi-sort-order requests against the Rainforest API to pull a larger, more varied sample within free-tier limits
  • Sentiment trends — tracks how rating and complaint rate move over time
  • Analysis — trusted reviews go to Claude in structured calls that return a strict JSON brief: complaint themes, buyer personas, risk alerts, keyword gaps, and a competitor gap analysis

Results

  • ~25% of reviews filtered out as untrustworthy on average per product, before any AI sees them
  • Full strategy brief in ~90 seconds, versus roughly 5 hours to read 500 reviews by hand
  • Competitor gap analysis that correctly surfaces cross-product strengths and weaknesses from review language

Reflections

A working AI pipeline is mostly about what happens around the model: filtering untrustworthy input before it's ever seen, forcing the output into a strict schema, and being honest about where the numbers stop being reliable — complaint percentages get shaky below about 50 reviews per product.
